# Evidence that composition fails with how rarely two concepts appear together

This note collects what the literature has measured about one question: does product-of-experts composition, or a joint two-object prompt, fail more often when the two concepts rarely appear together in the training data? It exists so that rung 11b of the ladder in the [simple-distributions rebuild guide](../../drips/simple-distributions-rebuild/PROMPTS.md) (a small text-to-image model trained on curated cat and dog photos, with the share of captions naming both set by hand) is designed around what is already known. The papers fall into three groups. Group 1 measures success against training-set frequency on large models. Group 2 gives a mechanism for multi-object failure on large models. Group 3 trains small models on curated data with the combinations controlled by hand.

Fetched for this run: 21 abstracts, number-bearing sections for 12 papers through the arXiv HTML pages, and the full text of one (Lovering and Pavlick, via the PDF). Papers judged in earlier runs and listed in the reading profile were not re-fetched; their rows say so. Semantic Scholar was not used. "Abstract-only" in the last column means no number was read from the paper body.

## Words this uses

**Co-occurrence share**: the fraction of training captions (or images) that name or contain both concepts. DOS measures it as a Jaccard index: captions naming both divided by captions naming at least one.

**Held-out combination**: a pairing of two concepts that never appears in training, while each concept appears on its own.

**One factor at a time**: the comparison changes exactly one property of the training data or model. "Observational" means nothing was changed by hand; the paper reads a correlation from existing data.

## What the evidence says

**The direct large-model measurement is thin.** One paper correlates per-prompt success with a counted LAION frequency on a text-to-image model: Lovering and Pavlick, r = 0.421 on Stable Diffusion 2.1, for subject-verb-object relations. No paper plots two-object compose rate on SDXL against the LAION co-occurrence of the pair. DOS has both halves (SDXL success by pair type and LAION Jaccard by pair) and stops short of joining them. No paper at any scale relates product-of-experts or score-sum composition to co-occurrence.

**The controlled studies agree on direction and disagree on what matters.** Okawa finds about 10 images of a class are enough for a colour change to appear, with a sharper threshold for shape. Liang finds single-concept "stripe" images make composition cheaper. Jeong finds held-out combinations fail more as more are held out, and that frequency skew matters less than scene complexity. None of them uses a text encoder with natural captions, and all use rendered shapes.

## What this means for rung 11b

**The dial is unmeasured, so 11b is new work.** Nobody has trained a text-to-image model on real photos with the share of both-name captions set by hand and read the compose rate along that share. The closest designs are binary (a pair is held out or it is not) or count one class, with tuple conditioning.

**Sweep the share on a log scale with zero and the low end dense.** Okawa's onset near 10 images and Liang's percolation threshold both say the change happens at small counts. Shares such as 0, 1 in 1,000, 1 in 100, 1 in 20 and 1 in 5 put more points where the curve is expected to bend than an even grid would.

**Hold the single-concept images fixed while the share moves.** Liang shows the single-concept images change composition on their own. If adding both-name images also changes the number of cat-only and dog-only images, or the total, two axes move at once. Keep the cat-only and dog-only sets identical across runs and add or remove only the both-name set, and print the per-run counts.

**Keep the image content of the both-name set fixed in kind.** Jeong finds scene complexity outweighs concept imbalance. Two-animal photos are busier than single-animal ones, so the share and the complexity move together unless the both-name images are chosen to match the single images in size and clutter. Say which way this goes when it cannot be matched.

**Freeze one text encoder for every run.** Zarei shows the SD 1.4 U-Net binds colour far better when only the CLIP text embedding is improved (VQA colour 0.36 to 0.75 with the U-Net frozen). If the encoder trains, it is a second variable. The product-of-experts arm prompts each animal alone, so it never asks the encoder to bind two names; the joint-prompt arm does. Report the two arms separately for that reason.

**Read the failure kind as well as the rate.** Jeong's two-object variant collapses to one object; Kamb and Ganguli predict patch mosaics from locality; this project's toys produce overlays and chimeras. A compose rate alone cannot tell these apart, so the scorer should label one animal, two animals and a hybrid as separate outcomes, as the project's composition calls already do with hybrid as its own category.

**What would be a surprise.** If the product-of-experts compose rate does not change across the share sweep while the joint-prompt rate does, the score-sum failure is not a co-occurrence effect at this scale. Nothing in the papers above predicts that outcome either way, which is why the run is worth doing.
