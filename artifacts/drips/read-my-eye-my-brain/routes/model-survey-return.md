# Route return: existing deployed models and the closest baseline for Sam

One line: the five named systems are real, published, and clinically validated, but published African-population evidence exists for only two of them (SELENA+ in Zambia and Tanzania, Google ARDA nowhere found), and the single closest existing work to "Sam" is Bellemo et al. 2019, a Singapore-trained ensemble CNN validated end to end on Zambian patients. The SAHPRA document is real, publicly posted, and primary-sourced below; it is a non-binding industry communication, not a registration pathway.

## 1. The five named systems

**IDx-DR**

Architecture, as publicly documented through its FDA submission: a client-server pipeline (IDx-DR Client, IDx-DR Service, IDx-DR Analysis System) rather than a single disclosed network architecture; the analysis system grades image quality and disease severity server-side. Quoted: "IDx-DR Analysis... analyzes patient images and determines exam quality" ([FDA 510(k) summary, K213037](https://510k.innolitics.com/device/K213037)). It was the first FDA-authorized autonomous AI diagnostic device, cleared in April 2018. Paraphrased: the pivotal trial enrolled 900 patients at primary-care sites and reported sensitivity 87.2% and specificity 90.7% for detecting more-than-mild diabetic retinopathy ([mobihealthnews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/fda-permits-marketing-ai-software-autonomously-detects-diabetic-retinopathy), [healio](https://www.healio.com/news/ophthalmology/20181113/artificial-intelligence-zeroes-in-on-dr)). African-population validation: not found. The search returned no specific sub-Saharan African validation study of IDx-DR; one review notes AI screening systems generally "can increase detection rates... and improve access to care" in low-resource settings without naming an IDx-DR-specific African trial ([PMC review, implementation challenges](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12864748/)).

**EyeArt (Eyenuk)**

Architecture: not publicly documented at the network level in the sources found (Eyenuk does not disclose the CNN architecture in its public validation papers); the system is described functionally as an "Automated DR Detection System," version 2.1.0 in the cited trial. Paraphrased: a prospective multicenter study of 942 diabetic adults at 15 US primary-care and eye-care sites (April 2017 to May 2018) found EyeArt sensitivity of 96.4% for more-than-mild DR versus 27.7% for ophthalmologists' dilated exams on the same cohort, and a separate retrospective read of over 100,000 patient visits reported sensitivity 91.3% and specificity 91.1% for referable DR ([crstodayeurope.com](https://crstodayeurope.com/news/study-finds-eyenuk-artificial-intelligence-detects-diabetic-retinopathy-with-greater-sensitivity-than-dilated-exams/2481158/), [University of Wisconsin summary](https://www.ophth.wisc.edu/blog/2021/11/01/pivotal-evaluation-of-an-artificial-intelligence-system-for-autonomous-detection-of-referrable-and-vision-threatening-diabetic-retinopathy)). EyeArt analyzes 2 images per eye, typically without pupil dilation, versus the 10-image dilated ETDRS reference standard. African-population validation: not found in the searches run here.

**Medios AI (Remidio)**

Architecture, paraphrased: "composed of two different algorithms based on convolutional neural networks, one assessing image-quality and the other separating healthy images from images with referable DR," run offline on the smartphone itself ([PMC, device-agnostic AI for DR screening](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11874329)). It pairs with Remidio's smartphone-based fundus camera rather than a desktop retinal camera. Paraphrased validation: a Mumbai civic-dispensary study (JAMA Ophthalmology) reported sensitivity/specificity of 100%/88.4% for referable DR and 85.2%/92.0% for any DR, exceeding the FDA's pre-specified superiority endpoints; a separate 900-patient study found 83.3%/95.5% sensitivity/specificity for any DR; a Yucatán Peninsula field study of 248 patients compared Medios against EyeArt on 2,130 images ([eyewire.news](https://eyewire.news/news/study-reports-high-sensitivity-in-diabetic-retinopathy-screening-using-smartphones-and-automated-ai-in-primary-care)). African-population validation: not found for Medios AI specifically. The Rwanda smartphone-camera DR screening deployment found in this search used Orbis International's "Cybersight AI," not Medios AI, and a separate feasibility study (827 participants, four Rwandan diabetes clinics, March to June 2021) reported 33.2% referred and 99.5% satisfaction, without naming the underlying algorithm as Medios ([aop.org.uk](https://www.aop.org.uk/ot/news/2023/09/09/using-ai-to-screen-for-diabetic-retinopathy-in-rwanda)). Do not conflate Cybersight AI with Medios AI; the search did not confirm they are the same system.

**Google ARDA (Automated Retinal Disease Assessment)**

Architecture: not fully disclosed in the sources found here beyond "an automated retinal disease assessment algorithm" built on deep convolutional networks; the specific network family (Inception, as sometimes reported elsewhere) was not confirmed by any source returned in this search, so it is marked not found rather than asserted. Paraphrased validation: a postdeployment study at Aravind Eye Hospital, southern India (45 sites), compared ARDA's output against US-ophthalmologist-adjudicated grades on roughly 1% of fundus photographs and found sensitivity 97.0% (95% CI 92.6 to 99.2%) and specificity 96.4% (95% CI 95.7 to 97.0%) for severe non-proliferative or proliferative DR. ARDA has screened over one million patients across India, Thailand, and Australia (Aravind, Rajavithi Hospital, Lions Outback Vision), licensed to Forus Health, AuroLab, and Perceptra ([aiweekly.co](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/googles-arda-eye-ai-passes-one-million-patients-screened)). A separate report notes the tool "struggles in clinical setting" under field image-quality conditions, stopping short of a diagnosis on poor-quality images ([healthexec.com](https://healthexec.com/topics/precision-medicine/google-ai-tool-diabetic-retinopathy-struggles)). African-population validation: not found. No study in this search names an African deployment or validation cohort for ARDA; the deployments found are India, Thailand, and Australia only.

**SELENA+ (Singapore)**

Architecture, paraphrased: "a customized convolutional neural network," trained on roughly 500,000 retinal images, developed jointly by the Singapore National Eye Centre, the Singapore Eye Research Institute, and National University of Singapore computer scientists, to detect diabetic retinopathy, glaucoma, and age-related macular degeneration from one system ([MOH Singapore](https://www.moh.gov.sg/news-highlights/details/efficacy-of-the-selena-system)). It was tested against 10 external validation datasets varying by camera, race, age, and glycemic control, and published in JAMA. It holds Singapore HSA approval and a European CE mark. African-population validation: confirmed, the only one of the five with a direct hit. SELENA+ has a published 2021 validation study in Zambia (cited by name in the proposal's own §6.2 as Bellemo et al. 2019, which is in fact the system SELENA+ descends from / an earlier version of the same Singapore-trained pipeline), and EyRIS (the company commercializing SELENA+) formed a tripartite partnership with the London School of Hygiene and Tropical Medicine's International Centre for Eye Health and Tanzania's Ministry of Health specifically "to validate the use of Artificial Intelligence in screening for diabetic retinopathy in low-resource settings" ([nova-hub.com, Tanzania partnership](https://www.nova-hub.com/novanews/eyris-forms-tri-partite-partnership-to-bring-selena-to-tanzania/), [ICEH, LSHTM](https://iceh.lshtm.ac.uk/selecting-an-artificial-intelligence-system-for-diabetic-eye-screening-in-tanzania/)). SELENA+ also has South African regulatory approval, per the same source.

## 2. Open-source baselines a PhD student could actually run

**EfficientNet-B0 + ordinal regression head on APTOS 2019** (github.com/adarshcod30/Diabetic-Retinopathy-Detection, referenced via its HuggingFace model card). Detects: 5-class ICDR diabetic retinopathy severity grading. Trained on: APTOS 2019 Blindness Detection (Kaggle). Complexity: EfficientNet-B0, roughly 5 million parameters, a standard CNN backbone runnable on a single consumer GPU; paraphrased, it reports a referable-DR AUC of 0.9242 on an external locked test set (Messidor-2 + IDRiD) ([HuggingFace model card](https://huggingface.co/adarshcod30/drdetect-dr-screening)).

**APTOS 2019 Kaggle-competition pipeline** (github.com/abhuse/aptos-retinopathy-detection). Detects: the same 5-class DR severity task. Trained on: APTOS 2019. Complexity: a from-scratch reimplementation of a Kaggle-leaderboard EfficientNet training pipeline with cross-validation support, i.e. the kind of pipeline that placed well in the original 2019 competition rather than a novel architecture ([GitHub](https://github.com/abhuse/aptos-retinopathy-detection)).

**RETFound** (github.com/rmaphoh/RETFound_MAE). Detects: a general-purpose retinal foundation model, fine-tunable for diabetic retinopathy, glaucoma, and other eye/systemic diseases, not a single-task classifier. Trained on: 1.6 million unlabeled retinal images via masked-autoencoder self-supervision, then fine-tuned per task on labeled sets including IDRiD, APTOS 2019, DeepDRiD (for DR) and AIROGS, PAPILA (for glaucoma). Complexity: ViT-Large backbone, 24 transformer blocks, 1,024-dim embeddings, meaning it needs a GPU with real memory headroom for fine-tuning, though a lighter "RETFound-Green" variant claims comparable performance at roughly 400x less compute and half the data ([arXiv 2405.00117](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.00117), [GitHub](https://github.com/rmaphoh/RETFound_MAE)). This is the heaviest of the three but is the one that actually spans both diseases Sam targets (DR and glaucoma) in one released codebase.

Not found: a specific open, runnable, glaucoma-only repository with a disclosed architecture and dataset. The glaucoma literature returned in this search (ResNet-50/ResNet-18/InceptionV3 hybrids on G1020, DRISHTI, ORIGA, PAPILA, REFUGE1, ACRIMA) is almost entirely published-paper results, not maintained public code; RETFound is the one glaucoma-capable option with a real repository.

## 3. The single best "closest existing work" for Sam

**Bellemo, V., Lin, Z., Sonka, M., et al. (2019). "Artificial intelligence using deep learning to screen for referable and vision-threatening diabetic retinopathy in Africa: a clinical validation study." *The Lancet Digital Health*, 1(1), e35-e44.** DOI/source confirmed via search: published in *The Lancet Digital Health*, May 2019 ([DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/943af08e6a1c42458a838c9a61eaa7f5)); paraphrased architecture and method, an ensemble of two CNNs, an adapted VGGNet and a residual network, trained on 76,370 retinal images from 13,099 diabetic patients in the Singapore Integrated Diabetic Retinopathy Program (2010 to 2013), then validated end to end, with zero African images in training, on 4,504 fundus images from 1,574 Zambian patients, finding "clinically acceptable performance for identifying referable retinopathy" ([AAO EyeNet summary](https://www.aao.org/eyenet/article/using-ai-to-screen-diabetic-retinopathy-in-africa)).

Why this one over the others: it is the only candidate in this survey that already states, measures, and publishes the exact transfer Sam proposes to make, a model trained entirely outside Africa, tested on an African population with no retraining, with a named sensitivity/specificity gap the proposal can cite as its starting baseline rather than inventing one.

## 4. The SAHPRA AI/ML communication (MD08-2025/2026)

Confirmed by direct primary-source extraction from the published PDF ([sahpra.org.za](https://www.sahpra.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/MD08-20252026_-SAHPRA-Communication-to-Industry-AI-Medical-devices_Acknowledgements.pdf)): this is a "Communication to Stakeholders," issue MD08-2025/2026, dated 26 September 2025, not a regulation or a registration pathway. Quoted: "SAHPRA has not commenced with product registration of medical devices and IVDs as per regulation 8 of the general regulations for medical devices including IVDs... the rapid advancement and adoption of such technologies necessitate proactive engagement with the medical device industry." It defines an AI/ML-enabled medical device as any device "that conforms to the definition of a medical device and utilises one or more AI or machine-learning algorithms to perform, in part or in whole, its intended medical purpose," covering medical imaging analysis, predictive algorithms, clinical decision support, and wearables, explicitly including diagnostic imaging software of the kind Sam would be. It states manufacturers must run a QMS (ISO 13485), risk management (ISO 14971, IEC 62304) covering AI-specific risks such as "algorithm errors, data drift," demonstrate clinical performance "through appropriate validation studies," and build in post-market performance monitoring for drift. It sets out five guiding principles (patient safety and risk management, transparency and explainability, cybersecurity/data integrity/privacy under POPIA, performance monitoring and adaptability, and clinical evaluation), the last of which directly names subgroup validation: quoted, "developers should conduct subgroup analyses to confirm that the device performs reliably across different demographic groups (e.g., across sexes, ages, ethnicities). Any biases or limitations observed in performance should be transparently acknowledged." It also states every AI/ML device, software-only or embedded, "shall require authorisation from SAHPRA before it can be made available for use in the South African population," via a medical device establishment licence under Section 22C of the Medicines and Related Substances Act, even though product-level registration itself has not yet commenced. In short, the document asks for exactly the kind of demographic subgroup evidence Sam's proposal is built around, but it creates no product-registration pathway yet, since South Africa has not activated medical-device product registration under Regulation 8 at all.

## Changes for the walk

The proposal's sentence 2 claim of a "near-total absence of AI diagnostic models... validated on African populations" needs qualifying, not deleting: it holds for IDx-DR, EyeArt, Medios AI, and Google ARDA (no published African validation found for any of the four in this search), but it does not hold for SELENA+, which has a 2019/2021 Zambian validation and an active Tanzania field-validation partnership, and the proposal's own §6.2 citation of Bellemo et al. 2019 is in fact that same Zambian SELENA-lineage validation, so the "near-total absence" framing and the Bellemo citation sit in slight tension with each other and should be reconciled in the same paragraph.

The "lightweight" model question in §5.1 has a concrete answer to weigh against: RETFound (ViT-Large, 1.6M-image pretraining) is the only open repository found that already covers both DR and glaucoma, but it is not lightweight; EfficientNet-B0 baselines are lightweight but DR-only and would need a separate glaucoma head or a second model entirely. Sam's architecture choice is a real fork between "one heavy multi-disease foundation model" and "two small single-disease models," and the proposal should say which it intends before citing feasibility.
