What each label and number means
    Where these numbers come from

    The toy. One Gaussian blob per quadrant at (±0.5, ±0.5), standard deviation 0.15. A is x1 > 0 (pneumothorax), B is x2 > 0 (no chest drain). The experts are ideal: blob mixtures with chosen weights, no training and no sampler. Every share is the composed density's mass inside a region, computed on a 441 × 441 grid over [−1.1, 1.1]² by the walk's answer key, artifacts/ideas/when-poe-reaches-a-and-b/runs/answer-key-quadrants/answer_key.py (output answer_key.json, run 2026-10-17 in the co3 environment).

    The regions. "In a blob's circle" is within 3 standard deviations (0.45) of that blob's centre. "At the origin" is within 3 · 0.15/√2 = 0.32 of (0, 0), the width of the blend two blobs multiply into. The origin circle and the four blob circles overlap a little, so a row's shares can add to slightly more than 1 (at ε = 0: 0.989 + 4 × 0.006).

    What 0.989 is. A 3-std circle holds 1 − e⁻⁴·⁵ = 0.989 of a blob with the data's width. So 0.989 means the whole blob. A product of a blob with itself is narrower (std 0.15/√2 = 0.106), so its circle holds about 0.9999 of it. That is why the shares on a squared mixture read 0.250 rather than 0.247.

    "Zero" on a Gaussian. A Gaussian is never exactly zero. Expert A's density at the top-right centre is 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁰ of its peak, which is what the zero rule means on this toy. Because the tails never quite vanish, the product of the two from-scratch experts is a real density (total mass Z = 7.9 × 10⁻¹⁰, the overlap of two blobs √2 apart), and after rescaling it sits at the origin.

    Values the walk's key did not list. The sliders stop at more values of ε than the key printed (0.2, 0.15, 0.075, 0.03, 0.02, 0.005, and 1e-6 for the next-door case; r = 10⁻⁴, 10⁻⁵, 10⁻⁶ and 10⁻⁸) and the rescue (b) frame quotes the spread along x1. These come from artifacts/scenes/three-ways-a-product-reaches-a-and-b/scene_key.py, which reproduces every row of the walk's key to a relative gap of 0 before adding them (printout scene_key.out). The shading on the picture is each density divided by its own peak, so it shows shape only; how much mass there is before rescaling is the Z chip.

    What A and B mean in the numbers. A is the right half and B the top half, but the key labels whole blobs: a film from the bottom-right blob counts as A even when its Gaussian tail crosses x1 = 0. Requiring the exact half-planes would mean indicator functions in p(x, A) and p(x, B), and a recomputed key. The four quadrant names on the picture name the blobs.

    Two kinds of expert. In frames 1 and 2, pA and pB are separately trained densities. In frame 3, p(x | A) and p(x | B) are conditionals of one model. They are different objects: from scratch, pA holds only bottom-right, while p(x | A) holds top-right and bottom-right.

    Which Z. A normaliser belongs to one written-out density, never to a proportionality. Frame 3's Z is the integral of qr = p(x, A) p(x, B)/p(x). The conditional form p(x | A) p(x | B)/p(x) equals qr/(P(A) P(B)), which rescales to the same composed density with a different integral. Z ≈ r holds while the blobs are far apart; at r = 0 the grid gives 3.8 × 10⁻⁷.

    The ε/(2 − 4ε) and 0.5 lines. Both drop the cross terms between different corners, which are 1.5 × 10⁻⁵ (corners 1 apart) or 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁰ (√2 apart) of a same-corner term. The grid keeps them. The 0.5 ceiling is exact without dropping anything: x → −x turns pA into pB, so the product is unchanged and the top-right and neither circles hold equal shares.

    Rescue (b)'s background. The background marginal on each coordinate is half at +0.5 and half at −0.5. The two training sets alone (bottom-right and top-left films) give exactly that marginal, so no top-right film is needed anywhere.
