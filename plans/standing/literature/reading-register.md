# The reading register

One row per paper read for this project. Every claim cited in `paper/iclr/iclr2027_conference.tex`
must trace to a row here; a claim needing a source that isn't below gets the `/paper-scout` cue in
the draft-section round, not a guess.

Each row's arXiv id and author list is checked directly against the arXiv abstract page, not
carried from memory or recall. "Read" states honestly how deep that check went: an abstract-level
check confirms the paper exists and says roughly what it's cited for; a deep read (marked where an
earlier session did one) is what backs a specific number, lemma, or mechanism claim.

## Read, considered, not selected

Each of these was fetched and judged during the 2026-08-18 scouting runs and kept for a named
reason. None is cited, so none has a row above.

- **Generative Modeling by Estimating Gradients of the Data Distribution, arXiv 1907.05600 (NeurIPS
  2019 Oral)**: Song and Ermon, metadata verified 2026-08-27. Considered for the score citation
  section 2 ¶3 needs and not selected, because it trains a network to output the score directly and never uses
  noise prediction, so it cannot state a relation between the two. It earns a row only if ¶3 grows
  a sentence saying that modelling the score is a generative paradigm in its own right.
- **GenAI-Bench, arXiv 2406.13743**: the one paper naming counting, negation and attribute binding
  as skills, over 80,000 human ratings. Its "Attribute" tag means visual properties of one object
  rather than binding across two, so it gives no binding failure rate. Held as the archival swap
  for 2411.17066 on negation. Its per-skill numbers were read through a summarizer, so nothing may
  be quoted from it without opening Appendix C Table 6.
- **HRS-Bench, arXiv 2304.05390 (ICCV 2023)**: the venue-published backup for the counting claim if
  2307.06350's numeracy protocol fails the check recorded in its row.
- **GenEval, arXiv 2310.11513 (NeurIPS 2023 D&B)**: cleaner than T2I-CompBench for counting alone
  but gives no binding detail and no negation, so it would cost an extra citation key. GenEval 2
  (2512.16853) argues it has saturated and drifted from human judgment, which a 2026 reviewer may
  raise.
- **Marigold, arXiv 2312.02145 (CVPR 2024)**: the archival-venue swap for 2306.05720 on physical
  structure. Its argument is indirect: geometry survives fine-tuning on synthetic data only, so it
  came from the pretrained prior.
- **Probing the 3D Awareness of Visual Foundation Models, arXiv 2404.08636 (CVPR 2024)**: covers
  visual foundation models rather than text-to-image specifically, and its headline is the
  boundary, that single-view 3D structure is encoded while cross-view consistency is not. Held for
  a rebuttal that has to concede that boundary, not as a citation for the claim.
- **PhyBench, arXiv 2406.11802**: tests 700 physical-commonsense prompts and finds text-to-image
  models lack physical reasoning outside optics. This is why the physical-structure sentence claims
  structure and not dynamics.
- **Compositional Visual Generation and Inference with Energy Based Models, arXiv 2004.06030
  (NeurIPS 2020 Spotlight)**: the origin of the composition line, but its combinatorial premise is
  framed about human cognition rather than training-set coverage, so it is weaker than 2402.01103
  for that citation. Reasonable as a second key alongside it if the sentence wants the lineage.

## Dropped, not a real match

  described this as a "two-basin (composition vs chimera), search-vs-shortcut" paper. The arXiv id
  actually resolves to "Parallel Tempering Initial Sampling in Inference-Time Reward Alignment"
  (Oh, Kim, Lee), about reward-alignment SMC sampling, with no composition or chimera content and
  no two-basin framing. Checked 2026-08-12. Left out rather than cited on a wrong id. If the real
  "two-basin" paper is found, add it here with its actual arXiv id.

> A basin is the set of starting points that all end up at the same kind of outcome. Two basins
> means composition and chimera are separate destinations a run falls into, rather than two ends of
> one sliding scale.
